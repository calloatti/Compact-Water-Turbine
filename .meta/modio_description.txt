<p>&quot;Beavers soon realized that if a pump uses power to move water, water can move a pump to make power.&quot;</p>
<p>The Compact Water Turbine is a highly efficient, 1x1 mechanical power generator designed for precision engineering and tight settlement layouts. Instead of relying on massive, slow-moving water wheels, this turbine harnesses the pressure of falling water to generate scalable mechanical power.</p>
<h3 id="key-features">Key Features</h3>
<p>Dynamic Water Physics: The visual water stream physically reacts to your network. At 100% capacity, water shoots from the pipe in a heavy, pressurized arc. As the flow dies down, the stream dynamically loses pressure, thinning out and dropping straight down into a dribble.
Flow-Proportional Power: Power output isn't just on or off. It scales dynamically based on two factors: the volume of water moving through the turbine, and the height of the drop (head pressure).
The water flow can be adjusted from 0 to 0.5 m3/s</p>
<h3 id="technical-specs">Technical Specs</h3>
<p>Maximum Flow Rate: 0.5 m3s (Cubic Meters per Second)
Minimum Head to work: 1.0 meters (Turbine will stop working when head &lt; 1 m)
Minimum Head to start: 1.6 meters (Turbine will not start when head &lt; 1.6 m)
Maximum Drop: 5 meters (Power generation maxes out at a 5m drop)
Maximum Power generation: 1000 hp at 0.5 m3s and 5.0 m or greater head</p>
<h3 id="how-the-head-is-measured">How the head is measured</h3>
<p>The turbine simply takes the effective water height at the intake and subtracts the effective water height at the exhaust.</p>
<h3 id="how-to-use">How to Use</h3>
<p>Simply place the Compact Water Turbine at the edge of a canal or dam. Ensure the intake is submerged and there is at least a 1-block drop from the intake water level to the output water level. Connect your mechanical shafts to the housing, and let the gravity do the rest!</p>
<h3 id="water-turbine-head-definition">Water Turbine Head Definition</h3>
<p>The head of a water turbine is the height difference between the water entering the turbine and the water leaving it. It is a critical factor in determining the turbine's efficiency and the amount of power it can generate.</p>

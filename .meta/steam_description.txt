"Beavers soon realized that if a pump uses power to move water, water can move a pump to make power."

The Compact Water Turbine is a highly efficient, 1x1 mechanical power generator designed for precision engineering and tight settlement layouts. Instead of relying on massive, slow-moving water wheels, this turbine harnesses the pressure of falling water to generate scalable mechanical power.

[h3]Key Features[/h3]
Dynamic Water Physics: The visual water stream physically reacts to your network. At 100% capacity, water shoots from the pipe in a heavy, pressurized arc. As the flow dies down, the stream dynamically loses pressure, thinning out and dropping straight down into a dribble. Flow-Proportional Power: Power output isn't just on or off. It scales dynamically based on two factors: the volume of water moving through the turbine, and the height of the drop (head pressure). The water flow can be adjusted from 0 to 0.5 m3/s

[h3]Technical Specs[/h3]
Maximum Flow Rate: 0.5 m3s (Cubic Meters per Second) Minimum Head to work: 1.0 meters (Turbine will stop working when head < 1 m) Minimum Head to start: 1.6 meters (Turbine will not start when head < 1.6 m) Maximum Drop: 5 meters (Power generation maxes out at a 5m drop) Maximum Power generation: 1000 hp at 0.5 m3s and 5.0 m or greater head

[h3]How the head is measured[/h3]
The turbine simply takes the effective water height at the intake and subtracts the effective water height at the exhaust.

[h3]How to Use[/h3]
Simply place the Compact Water Turbine at the edge of a canal or dam. Ensure the intake is submerged and there is at least a 1-block drop from the intake water level to the output water level. Connect your mechanical shafts to the housing, and let the gravity do the rest!

[h3]Water Turbine Head Definition[/h3]
The head of a water turbine is the height difference between the water entering the turbine and the water leaving it. It is a critical factor in determining the turbine's efficiency and the amount of power it can generate.
